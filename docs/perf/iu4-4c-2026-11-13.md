# IU4 4c: kept batches drawn in their scroll frames

`vapors-perf 60 10 --instructions --only "<n> rows, <workload>"`,
release, one run per build, median instructions per frame in millions.
Before: 4c-i (#266) with the `batches` draw step, so both columns
split it out. After: 4c-ii's head. Buffer bytes and batch counts are
the last frame's. Load average 4 to 18 during the runs. Instruction
counts do not move with load.

- Scroll: 5 of 53 batches are written per frame, the rows entering and
  leaving at the view's edges; the other 48 are drawn from kept bytes,
  moved by the frame's shift in the uniform. Write falls from 0.72 to
  0.10 million instructions.
- Insert: the workload inserts outside the view. Paint order is
  walked, so the steps are sorted again. (An insert inside the view
  moves the rows below it within their frame, and those rows' batches
  are written, as with Resize.) The batches made again keep
  every key, and nothing is written.
- Resize: the rows below the resized second row truly move, so their
  batches are written again; 2 more are kept than before.
- What still grows with the rows on Scroll and Insert is the `batches`
  step: the steps are sorted again from every drawing step
  (`KeptBatches::of`, O(steps + items)), about 1,170 instructions per
  row at 10,000 rows. IU4 PR 5's extents index takes this. Outside
  `batches`, Scroll's draw is 1.27 million instructions at 10,000 rows
  against Idle's 0.93; the difference is building and writing the 5
  edge batches. So 4c's acceptance (Scroll's draw equal to Idle's
  within noise at 3000 and 10,000 rows) holds only with PR 5.
